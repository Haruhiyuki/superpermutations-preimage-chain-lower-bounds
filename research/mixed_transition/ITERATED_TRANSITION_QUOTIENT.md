# Iterated transition quotients

## Tail conjugacy

For the overlap transition

\[
T_k(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=(x_{k+1},\ldots,x_n,x_k,\ldots,x_1),
\]

fix `r≥1` and write a permutation as `(a_1,…,a_r,u)`. Whenever `r+j≤n`, direct
calculation gives

\[
T_r^{-1}T_{r+j}(a_1,\ldots,a_r,u)
=(a_1,\ldots,a_r,T_j(u)).
\]

Thus, after quotienting by a baseline transition of weight `r`, an additional `j` units of overlap
cost act only on the tail. The first `r` symbols are frozen.

Important special cases are

\[
T_1^{-1}T_2=\operatorname{id}_1\times T_1,
\qquad
T_2^{-1}T_3=\operatorname{id}_2\times T_1.
\]

The first identity explains why every balanced `T₁/T₂` modification decomposes into complete
2-cycles. The second explains why runs of weight-three kernel splices have period `n-2`.

## Consequence for general constructions

This suggests a hierarchy of quotient problems:

1. quotient by the current baseline transition `T_r`;
2. classify balanced alternating cycles in the tail action generated by `T_j`;
3. lift a tail gadget back to the full permutation graph;
4. repeat at the next scale.

The gain-one complete-2-cycle forest is the level-1 quotient. The uniform score-`2(n-2)` kernel in
`UNIFORM_SCORE_TWO_KERNEL.md` is a level-2 mixed-transition object: it contains weight-three
structure internally rather than merely using weight three between completed components.

A scalable gain larger than two would require a family of alternating exchange cycles that survives
this quotient recursively. In particular, a useful gadget should have a symbolic tail description
rather than a list of degree-specific permutations.

## Exchange certificate

Let `F` be any bijective successor on `S_n`. An alternating exchange certificate consists of
sources `p_1,…,p_m` and new weights `k_1,…,k_m` satisfying

\[
T_{k_i}(p_i)=F(p_{i+1})
\]

with cyclic indices. Replacing the outgoing `F`-edge of each `p_i` by the displayed edge preserves
bijectivity. The exact cost change is

\[
\Delta=\sum_i k_i-\sum_i w_F(p_i).
\]

After applying tail conjugacy, the certificate can often be represented entirely on a smaller
alphabet. This is the proposed certificate format for a recursive mixed-transition construction and
for later Lean verification.

## Research target

The decisive general theorem would have the following form.

> There exists a symbol-stable family of tail exchange certificates `G_n`, compatible with the
> `n→n+1` lift, whose total score is at least `q(n-2)` for some `q≥2`, and whose remaining cyclic
> classes admit a rooted complete-2-cycle completion.

The uniform kernel family proves the kernel part for `q=2`. The remaining problem is the rooted
completion and its lifting law.
